# License FAQ

This FAQ is a practical explanation of Pico's licensing intent. It is not a replacement for `LICENSE`, `NOTICE` or `COMMERCIAL.md`.

Pico is source-available for private and non-commercial use under the PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0 with Pico-specific additional permissions. Business use is additionally permitted on a self-operated Pico Home.

Commercial Pico hosting remains reserved to the designated Pico rights holder. Any other commercial use requires prior written permission.

The short version: you may put Pico to work in your own business, as long as you run the Pico Home yourself. You may not run Pico Homes for other people as a business.

## What is allowed without commercial permission?

The following uses are allowed without additional commercial permission:

- private use by one person
- private Pico Home use
- private Pico WG use
- family or household use
- private shared-home use
- private non-commercial friend-group use
- hobby, study, experimentation and testing without anticipated commercial application
- private cost sharing that only reimburses actual shared infrastructure costs
- business use of one or more Picos on a self-operated Pico Home

## What is a self-operated Pico Home?

A Pico Home is self-operated when the person or entity using it holds custody of the Home's host keys, operates and controls the Home itself, and did not get that Home from someone else as commercial hosting, a managed service or part of a paid offering.

Renting a server, virtual machine, storage or connectivity is not by itself commercial Pico hosting. What matters is who operates the Pico Home and who holds its host keys, not who owns the metal.

## What is a private Pico WG?

A private Pico WG is a non-commercial shared Pico Home arrangement between people who personally share a household, family context, friendship context or similar private relationship.

A private Pico WG may share actual infrastructure costs such as electricity, storage or server hosting costs.

It must not become a paid hosting service, subscription, managed service, support business, SaaS offering or commercial service.

## Can a family use one Pico Home together?

Yes.

Family and household use is allowed as private non-commercial use.

## Can a shared flat use one Pico Home together?

Yes.

A private shared home may use Pico as a private Pico WG, including private cost sharing for actual infrastructure costs.

## Can friends run a shared Pico Home?

Yes, if it is private and non-commercial.

It must not be advertised as a public service, paid hosting, managed hosting or subscription.

## Can a guesthouse, workshop or shop use Pico?

Yes, on its own Pico Home.

A guesthouse can put one or more Picos to work for the business, and those Picos may interact with staff and with guests and customers. That the guests are paying customers changes nothing: it is still the guesthouse's own Pico doing the guesthouse's work.

What is not covered is running Picos for other people. Giving each guest a Pico or Pico Home of their own, operated by the guesthouse, is commercial Pico hosting and needs permission.

## Where exactly is the line for guests?

The question is not whether outsiders come into contact with Pico, and not whether money changes hands. It is whose companion is being run.

Permitted:

- a guest talks to the guesthouse's Pico
- a guest's own Pico communicates with the guesthouse's Pico
- the guesthouse's Pico handles bookings, questions or service tasks for paying guests

Requires permission:

- each guest gets their own Pico, run by the guesthouse on the guest's behalf
- guests are enrolled as members of the guesthouse's Pico Home
- "your own Pico Home included" is part of the room price

If the guesthouse would hold the keys to something that is meant to be the guest's own companion, that is hosting.

## Can a club, volunteer fire brigade or non-profit use Pico?

Yes, on its own Pico Home.

Operational use for official duties, member services, infrastructure operation and organisational administration is covered as long as the organisation operates the Pico Home itself.

Offering Pico Homes to members or third parties as a service still requires permission.

## Can a school, university or research group use Pico?

Yes, on its own Pico Home.

Study, teaching, research, experimentation and institutional operation are covered by self-operated business use.

Sponsored product development, paid services built on Pico and deployment as a hosted service for others require permission.

## Can a company use Pico internally?

Yes, on its own Pico Home.

Production use, use by employees, and use in the company's own workflows are permitted when the company operates the Pico Home itself and holds its host keys.

Selling Pico, embedding it in a commercial product, or offering it to customers as a service requires prior written permission.

## Can an IT service provider install Pico for someone?

Yes, and it may be paid for that.

Installing, configuring, migrating, maintaining, updating and supporting a customer's self-operated Pico Home is permitted.

The provider must not operate the Pico Home as its own service, hold the host keys as a service provider, or offer Pico Homes to its customers. Being paid to work on someone's Pico Home is allowed; being paid to be their Pico Home is not.

## Can someone offer paid Pico hosting?

No.

Paid Pico hosting, managed Pico Home services, Pico Home rental, multi-tenant Pico Home hosting for money, SaaS operation and operating a Pico Home on behalf of another party require prior written permission. This is the part that stays with the designated Pico rights holder.

## Can someone offer paid support?

Yes, for a Pico Home the customer operates itself.

Paid installation, maintenance and support around a self-operated Pico Home are permitted.

Managed operation, hosted offerings and anything where the provider ends up running the Pico Home still require prior written permission.

## Does a business need its own hardware?

No.

The test is operation and key custody, not hardware ownership. A rented server or virtual machine is fine as long as the business is the only party that operates the Pico Home and holds its host keys.

What is not allowed is obtaining the Pico Home itself from a third party as a hosted or managed service.

## Can someone accept donations?

Donations can be a grey area.

Occasional voluntary donations for a private non-commercial project may be acceptable if they do not create paid access, paid support, hosting obligations, subscriptions, service tiers, business advantage or profit.

Donation-funded public hosting, support obligations, paid tiers or service-style operation require permission.

## Can someone make videos, tutorials or articles about Pico?

Non-commercial tutorials, reviews and educational material are allowed.

Paid courses, commercial training, sponsored content, commercial consulting funnels or bundled support offerings may require permission.

## Can someone fork Pico?

Private and non-commercial modification is allowed within the license.

Compatibility claims are separate from licensing. A fork must not present itself as official Pico, official Pico Home, Pico Home Managed or Pico-compatible unless the applicable compatibility, naming and provenance requirements are met.

## Can a fork remove license restrictions?

No.

Forks must comply with the license and required notices.

## Does Pico grant trademark rights?

No.

The license does not grant trademark rights, brand rights, endorsement rights or official-service rights.

## Does Pico compatibility grant commercial permission?

No.

A compatible implementation still needs commercial permission for commercial use.

Compatibility is about protocol behaviour and user safety. It is not a commercial hosting license.

## What should I do if I am unsure?

If you run the Pico Home yourself, for yourself or for your own business, you are almost certainly covered.

Ask before offering Pico, a Pico Home or access to one to anybody else.
